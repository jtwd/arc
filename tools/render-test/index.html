Faces, and what the Frayed lost

Alegus has a brow, an eye that blinks, a nose and a mouth, and they change with what he is doing. The Frayed do not: an eye is a hole where an eye was and a mouth is the shape of one, which is the bible rather than a shortcut. Arrows move, space swings, shift dashes. The gate

Production plan, rung 2. If the combat does not feel good, the project stops or changes here, and that call is Edward's. It is far cheaper to find out now than in a year.

Is his face doing enough?

Brows up by default, because wonder is his register and one shape carries it. Down and mouth open on the active frames of a swing. Up and open when he is hit. He blinks, and he looks at the nearest Frayed.

Do they read as different animals?

The wolf has a deep chest and carries its head level. The boar is the opposite shape on purpose: a huge shoulder hump, tiny hindquarters, head low, and a bristle ridge that spikes the outline. If you cannot tell them apart at a glance, the silhouettes are wrong.

Does the club land?

A hit now holds the whole frame for a few hundredths of a second and squashes whatever it hit. Nothing else in a melee game buys as much weight for as little work. If it feels mushy, that number is one line.

Can you close on a hunter?

It holds range and backs away, and is only open during the long recovery after a throw. If closing on one is frustrating rather than satisfying, its numbers are wrong and I will change them.

What to tell me

Not "it is fine". Which enemy you hated, whether that was a good hate or a bad one, and whether you ever died feeling it was not your fault. Numbers are easy to change; the feel is the thing.
