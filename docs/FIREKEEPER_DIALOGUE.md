# The Firekeeper: the first ten conversations

Script for the vertical slice hub. One conversation unlocks per return to the Hearth, in order, and plays when Alegus approaches the fire. Each is under 45 seconds spoken. Alegus's lines follow `ALEGUS.md`; the Firekeeper's follow the notes below.

**The Firekeeper.** The fire itself, speaking from the flames. It never has a body. Its voice is low, unhurried, and kind, with the faint crackle of the fire underneath. It knows what Alegus is but tells him slowly, because it is not sure he wants to know. It never lies to him. It leaves things out. Casting: any gender, older than Alegus by decades in the voice, no accent that codes to a place.

The reveal that the Firekeeper is the fire Alegus carried is not in these ten. It is seeded in conversations 4, 7, and 10 and paid off at alpha.

---

## 1. First return

*Alegus wakes on the cave floor. The fire is the only light.*

**FIREKEEPER:** There you are.

**ALEGUS:** Where is here?

**FIREKEEPER:** The Hearth. It's where you come back to.

**ALEGUS:** I was on a lake. There was something enormous and it was hurt and it was angry about it.

**FIREKEEPER:** She's still there. She'll be there when you go back.

**ALEGUS:** Go back?

**FIREKEEPER:** You'll want to. You won't be able to help it. The cold gets in everything else, but not the wanting to see what's over the ridge. That's yours. That's what brought you here in the first place.

**ALEGUS:** I don't remember getting here.

**FIREKEEPER:** No. That's all right. Pick something up from the rack. Go and look.

---

## 2. Second return

**ALEGUS:** She killed me again.

**FIREKEEPER:** Mm.

**ALEGUS:** You don't sound surprised.

**FIREKEEPER:** She's been protecting the same thing for a very long time. She's good at it. You've been fighting for two days.

**ALEGUS:** Two days? It feels like longer.

**FIREKEEPER:** It is longer. It's also two days. Both of those are true here. Don't pull at it, it doesn't come apart in any useful way.

**ALEGUS:** What is she protecting?

**FIREKEEPER:** Listen, next time. Not to her. Under her.

---

## 3. Third return

*Unlocks the Cave Wall tutorial.*

**FIREKEEPER:** You've been bringing things back.

**ALEGUS:** The red earth. It's in my hands when I wake up. I didn't pick it up.

**FIREKEEPER:** You did. You just weren't holding it with your hands. Put some on the wall.

**ALEGUS:** Why?

**FIREKEEPER:** Because that's what it's for. People put their hands on walls so that the wall remembers. The wall's very old. It has a good memory. It'll give some back.

*(Alegus touches the wall. The first Cave Wall row appears.)*

**ALEGUS:** It's warm.

**FIREKEEPER:** Everything here is.

---

## 4. Fourth return

**ALEGUS:** The wolves aren't wolves.

**FIREKEEPER:** No.

**ALEGUS:** They look like wolves until they die and then they run. Like water. Like they were never held together properly.

**FIREKEEPER:** They weren't. They're Frayed. Something at the far end is pulling, and things come loose. The ones that come loose don't know what they are any more. They only know what they were for. A wolf was for hunting.

**ALEGUS:** And the big one? Her?

**FIREKEEPER:** She isn't Frayed. She's the opposite. She's holding on so hard she's the only solid thing on that lake.

**ALEGUS:** I carried something once. I remember carrying it. I don't remember what.

**FIREKEEPER:** *(a pause, the fire crackles)* You did.

---

## 5. Fifth return

*Plays after the first Mammoth Mother kill, whether it happens on return 5 or later. The ordering shifts so this is always the post-kill conversation.*

**ALEGUS:** She knelt.

**FIREKEEPER:** Yes.

**ALEGUS:** I didn't want her to. I thought I'd feel like I'd won. There was something under the ice and it stopped calling and she knelt down and the lake went. All of it went. Like I'd spilled it.

**FIREKEEPER:** You'll feel that again. Not every time. Enough times.

**ALEGUS:** Is that what you want from me? Spilling things?

**FIREKEEPER:** No. I want you to go and see what's past the lake. That's all I've ever wanted from you. The spilling is what it costs. I'm sorry that it does.

*(Alegus adds a handprint to his forearm. Scripted, visible.)*

**FIREKEEPER:** There. Now the wall isn't the only thing that remembers.

---

## 6. Sixth return

*Unlocks the Edge lineage on the rack.*

**FIREKEEPER:** There's a knife on the rack.

**ALEGUS:** There's always been a knife on the rack. It's been grey.

**FIREKEEPER:** It isn't now. You've earned a key. You don't have to understand what that means yet. It means the rack trusts you with something with an edge.

**ALEGUS:** Do you trust me with something with an edge?

**FIREKEEPER:** I trust you to try to talk to it first. That's usually the problem.

---

## 7. Seventh return

**ALEGUS:** What's past the lake?

**FIREKEEPER:** Warm stone. A river so wide you can't see the other bank. People who put their dead in the ground with bread so they won't be hungry.

**ALEGUS:** You've been.

**FIREKEEPER:** I've been everywhere you'll go. I was carried there.

**ALEGUS:** By who?

**FIREKEEPER:** By everyone. One at a time. *(beat)* Go and see the river, Alegus. It's the same water as the lake. It just got further.

---

## 8. Eighth return

*Plays after a death with Edge equipped, if it has happened; otherwise plays after any death.*

**ALEGUS:** I tried to talk to the bear.

**FIREKEEPER:** How did that go?

**ALEGUS:** Poorly. It didn't have a mouth any more. Just the shape of one. I think that was the worst part, that it was still trying to use it.

**FIREKEEPER:** They all still try. That's the thing about people, and about bears. Being pulled apart doesn't stop the trying. That's why they're dangerous, and it's why I don't hate them. You don't either. I've watched you not hate them.

**ALEGUS:** Should I?

**FIREKEEPER:** It would be easier. You won't, though. You're not built that way. You picked up something hot once when everyone else stayed back. Hating things isn't in a person who does that.

---

## 9. Ninth return

**ALEGUS:** The wall's giving me things back. More than I put in.

**FIREKEEPER:** It does that.

**ALEGUS:** Why?

**FIREKEEPER:** Because a hand on a wall is a promise to come back and look at it. The wall pays for promises. Everything here does. The rack. The fire. All of it runs on someone deciding to go and look again.

**ALEGUS:** And if I stopped?

**FIREKEEPER:** *(gently)* Then the far end would finish pulling, and it would be very quiet, and I'd sit here and remember you. I'd rather not. But it's yours to decide. It always has been. Nobody made you pick up the hot thing either.

---

## 10. Tenth return

*Plays after the second Mammoth Mother kill or the fifteenth run, whichever comes first. This is the slice's last conversation and the hook for alpha.*

**FIREKEEPER:** You've seen the lake enough times to know it now.

**ALEGUS:** I know the ice. I know where it breaks and where the wolves come from and how she leans when she's about to stamp. I know her better than I know myself. I don't know myself at all.

**FIREKEEPER:** You know one thing.

**ALEGUS:** I carried something.

**FIREKEEPER:** You did. And it got here, and you didn't, and the Loom caught you on the way down because it needed one thread that had been there at the start. That's you. That's all it is. You're the one who was there first.

**ALEGUS:** First for what?

**FIREKEEPER:** *(the fire rises, brief and bright)* Go and see the river. Then come back and I'll tell you what you carried.

*(End of slice content. In alpha, the Rivers age unlocks here.)*

---

## Production notes

- Ten conversations, roughly 90 Alegus lines and 70 Firekeeper lines.
- Conversations 5 and 8 have gating logic; the rest are strictly sequential. If the player kills the Mammoth Mother on run one, conversation 5 plays at return 2 and the others shift down.
- Each conversation should be skippable line by line but not as a whole on first play.
- The fire's animation should respond to the Firekeeper's lines: brighter on the last line of 10, lower and steadier through 9.
- Alegus should have a small idle set at the fire: warming hands, looking at his forearm, looking at the rack.
