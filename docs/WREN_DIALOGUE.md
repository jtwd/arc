# Wren: beats and in-run lines

Written early on purpose. `STORY_SCHEDULE.md` section 8 flags that Wren carries the late-game tone and sits last in the writing order, which is the worst possible combination. This is her drafted ahead of that.

Character and voice hook are in `CAST.md` section 3. The short version: total confidence, frequently wrong, never corrected, the inverse of Alegus.

---

## 1. What she knows, and what she does not

This is the engine for everything below and it is worth being precise about.

Wren is from the Age of Sail. She knows ships, rope, powder, charts, and weather **from having lived them**. Everything before her own age she knows the way a sailor of her century would: secondhand, garbled, and delivered with total confidence. She is the opposite of the Archivist, who has sources and no experience. She has experience of exactly one age and opinions about all of them.

So she is confidently wrong through ages 1 to 4, genuinely expert in age 5, and completely lost at the Terminus. That last one is where her defining line lands at launch scope. Post-launch it moves earlier, to the Age of Wars.

**She still runs.** She is out on the weave in her own runs, which is why her marks are fresh and why their paths cross. She has reached the Terminus and stood in it and been unable to do anything there, because the Loom needs a thread that was present at the start and she is from the middle. She is better at this than Alegus and it does not matter. That is the wound under the bragging.

---

## 2. The marks

Her in-run mechanic and, as it turns out, the whole character.

She drowned in calm water, at night, and nobody on the ship noticed until morning. She was eight hours gone before anyone said a word. She can make a joke of dying. She has never once made a joke of not being noticed.

So she carves her initial on everything. Every wall, every hull, every tomb ceiling. The mechanic in `CAST.md` where rooms occasionally carry a carved W and a scratched tally is not flavour. It is the character, and the payoff in beat 9 costs nothing to build because the marks already exist.

## 3. The beats

### Beat 1. Arrival (run 22)

*She is already at the Hearth, sitting where nobody sits, eating something that is not hers.*

**WREN:** You're the one who was there first.

**ALEGUS:** ...Yes?

**WREN:** Huh. Expected taller. Thirty-one rooms, by the way. That's mine. Best run anyone's put on this weave.

**ALEGUS:** I don't know how many rooms I've done.

**WREN:** Twenty-two. Your best is twenty-two.

**ALEGUS:** How do you know that?

**WREN:** Because I counted. Obviously I counted. What else am I going to do, sit down? *(she is sitting down)*

---

### Beat 2. The marks (first mark found in a run)

**ALEGUS:** There's a letter cut into the wall in the brickworks.

**WREN:** Is there.

**ALEGUS:** It's a W.

**WREN:** Is it.

**ALEGUS:** There's a number under it.

**WREN:** That'll be my time. You can't read numbers.

**ALEGUS:** I can read that one. It's got a lot of scratches.

**WREN:** It's a *good* time, is what it's got. Anyway. If you see more, they're all mine, don't get excited.

**ALEGUS:** Why would I get excited?

**WREN:** *(already leaving)* People do.

---

### Beat 3. The shared room (first co-op room)

*After a room they fought together.*

**WREN:** Nine.

**ALEGUS:** Nine what?

**WREN:** Nine of them, me. You had six. I'm not making a thing of it. I'm stating it. It's a fact and I'm stating it and then I'm going to keep stating it later.

**ALEGUS:** You took a hit for me.

**WREN:** I did not.

**ALEGUS:** The one with the shield. You stepped in front of it.

**WREN:** I *tripped*. **ALEGUS:** You tripped sideways. Into a shield.

**WREN:** *(handing him a length of knotted rope)* Take this and stop talking. It's for when you're about to die. Put it on. Then when you're about to die you'll run faster, which is the correct response to being about to die, and which you have never once done.

*(Wren's Knot unlocked.)*

---

### Beat 4. History, according to Wren (mid, any time after beat 3)

*Light comic beat. Exists to establish her as unreliable before the heavy beats land.*

**ALEGUS:** The men in the straight lines. With the red.

**WREN:** Romans.

**ALEGUS:** You know them?

**WREN:** Everyone knows Romans. They invented the road.

**ALEGUS:** The whole road?

**WREN:** The whole concept of the road. Before that people just went wherever. Chaos. Also they invented the bath, the alphabet, and I think the horse.

**ALEGUS:** The horse.

**WREN:** Don't look at me like that, I've been dead four hundred years, my sources are a man called Dunnet who I met once.

---

### Beat 5. How she died (gift, 2 Tallow)

*The pivot. She plays it as a joke and the joke does not work, and she knows it does not work, and she tells it anyway because the alternative is saying it plainly.*

**ALEGUS:** How did you get here?

**WREN:** Went over the side.

**ALEGUS:** In a storm.

**WREN:** No. That's the thing. Flat calm. Middle of the night, not a breath of wind, and I just went over. No reason. Feet did it, not me.

**ALEGUS:** Did they come back for you?

**WREN:** *(beat)* They didn't know.

**ALEGUS:** They didn't...

**WREN:** That's the funny bit. You'll like this bit. Nobody noticed till morning. Eight hours. I'd been gone eight hours before anyone said a single word about it. *(beat)* Ha.

**ALEGUS:** Wren.

**WREN:** Don't.

**ALEGUS:** I wasn't going to say anything.

**WREN:** You were going to say something with your face. Don't do that either.

---

### Beat 6. Her own age (first entry to the Age of Sail)

*The only place on the weave she has actually been. She goes quiet in a way that surprises him, because quiet is his thing, not hers.*

**ALEGUS:** The ground moved today.

**WREN:** Yeah.

**ALEGUS:** You're not going to tell me what it was?

**WREN:** It's a ship.

**ALEGUS:** I know it's a ship, you've said ship about ninety times. I mean you're not going to tell me *about* it. You tell me about everything. You told me about the horse.

**WREN:** *(long pause)* There's a smell. When the tar's warm and the rope's wet. It's the best smell there's ever been and I can't get at it any more, I just walk past where it was. *(beat)* Right. Anyway. Romans invented ships.

**ALEGUS:** They did not.

**WREN:** Prove it.

---

### Beat 7. She cannot finish it (after the first Terminus attempt)

**WREN:** You got to the white room.

**ALEGUS:** Not for long.

**WREN:** I got there. Did you know I got there? I got there before you.

**ALEGUS:** You've mentioned it. Eleven times.

**WREN:** Twelve. *(beat)* And I stood in it. And I did nothing. Because there's nothing in there for me to do. It doesn't want me. It wants the one from the start, and that's you, and it's always been you.

**ALEGUS:** Wren...

**WREN:** I'm faster than you. I'm better at this than you and I've been better at it the whole time and it doesn't matter *even slightly*. *(beat)* So hurry up. Because I can get there, and you can finish it, and those aren't the same thing and only one of them is any use.

---

### Beat 8. Both lost (first entry to the Terminus)

*The line the whole late-game tone plan rests on. Post-launch this beat moves to the Age of Wars, and the Terminus gets a quieter version.*

**ALEGUS:** What is it?

**WREN:** Don't know.

**ALEGUS:** You always know. You knew about the horse.

**WREN:** Yeah, well. *(beat)* Right. Well. I don't know what that one is either. Let's both not know it together.

**ALEGUS:** All right.

**WREN:** Stay close.

**ALEGUS:** You've never said that.

**WREN:** I've never meant it.

---

### Beat 9. Forty-one (after the first Terminus clear)

*The payoff. Nothing needs building for it: the marks are already in every room.*

**ALEGUS:** Forty-one.

**WREN:** Forty-one what.

**ALEGUS:** Your marks. I counted too. There's one in the tithe barn behind the door. One under the aqueduct where you have to crouch. One on the underside of the gun deck rail which I think you did upside down and badly.

**WREN:** *(nothing)*

**ALEGUS:** I noticed all of them.

**WREN:** ...Shut up.

**ALEGUS:** I'm going to keep noticing them.

**WREN:** I said shut up. *(beat)* Forty-two. You missed one.

**ALEGUS:** Where?

**WREN:** The tomb. In the dark. On the ceiling.

**ALEGUS:** Why the ceiling?

**WREN:** Because nobody looks up. *(beat)* I wanted it to be hard.

---

### Beat 10. Epilogue (rotating, after subsequent clears)

**WREN:** You're going again.

**ALEGUS:** I'm going again.

**WREN:** You know it doesn't end. You push it back and it comes forward and you push it back.

**ALEGUS:** I know.

**WREN:** *(standing up)* Good. Thirty-one rooms. Beat that or don't talk to me.

**ALEGUS:** I've done thirty-four.

**WREN:** *(already walking)* You've done nothing of the sort and I'm going to go and check, personally, right now, and when I'm wrong I won't say so.

## 4. In-run lines

### 4.1 Mark barbs (30 lines)

Rare. A one-line voice barb left at a mark, as if recorded in the stone.

- "Beat that."
- "Took me four minutes. You've been here nine."
- "If you're reading this you're going too slow."
- "There's a better way round. I'm not telling you."
- "This one took three goes. Don't tell anyone."
- *(in the Age of Sail only, and the only sincere one)* "...I was here. I was actually here."

### 4.2 Shared room (40 lines)

Combat barks for the co-op room, plus a result line for each outcome.

- "Left! Your left! Your *other* left!"
- "That's four."
- "I'm not helping, I just want them dead before you get them."
- On Alegus taking a heavy hit: "Up. Get up. Get *up*."
- Her winning: "Nine to six. Say it back to me."
- Him winning: "Fluke. Statistical fluke. Happens."
- A tie: "No. Absolutely not. Someone's miscounted and it's you."

### 4.3 Reactions to player state (25 lines)

- On Alegus holding a Stone Age form: "You're carrying a rock. Into a castle. On purpose."
- On a long win streak: "You're getting good. It's upsetting."
- On repeated deaths to one boss: "It's the same one, isn't it. It's the same one every time. I'm not laughing. I'm going to laughabout it later but I'm not laughing now."
- On the Firekeeper's Hand being on: *(nothing, ever. She never mentions it. This is a deliberate silence, not a missing line.)*

### 4.4 The Stubborn Thread line

*Fires once, on a Stubborn Thread clear. She has obviously been saving it.*

**WREN:** You did the whole thing with the rock.

**ALEGUS:** I did.

**WREN:** The whole weave. Every age. With the first rock you ever picked up, and you never once let go of it.

**ALEGUS:** That's right.

**WREN:** *(beat)* That's the stupidest thing anyone has ever done and I've been dead four hundred years. *(beat)* I'm so proud of you I could be sick.

## 6. Rules for writing more of her

1. **She is wrong about everything before the Age of Sail, and right about the Age of Sail.** The comedy and the one moment of sincerity come from the same rule.
2. **She never finishes a sincere sentence without deflecting**, except three times: beat 7, beat 9, and the Stubborn Thread line. Those three land because every other time she has swerved.
3. **She never mentions the Firekeeper's Hand.** Not to tease, not to reassure. Silence from the loudest character in the game is the kindest thing available to her.
4. **Her jokes stay funny as the ages darken.** She is the exception to the gradient in `TONE.md` section 3, and she is the reason the gradient does not become misery. Do not thin her out along with Alegus.
5. **She always has a number.** Rooms, times, kills, years dead. If a line of hers could carry a number, it does.
