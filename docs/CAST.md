# The cast: Cartographer, Archivist, Wren, and keepsakes

The Firekeeper is covered in `FIREKEEPER_DIALOGUE.md`. This document is the other three, plus the keepsake system.

All of them fell out of the weave and live at the edge of it. None of them can run it. That is the thing they have in common and the reason they all want something from Alegus.

---

## 1. The Cartographer

**Function.** Sells thread overrides, door rerolls, and run modifiers for Flint and Charts. The shop between the Hearth and the run.

**Who.** They map the weave. The map is a table, and the table is part of them: they are grown into it at the waist and have not stood up in longer than they will say. They sell routes because they cannot walk them. Alegus is their legs and they will never once say so.

**The voice hook: they speak only in directions and distances.** Not as a quirk they drop when it matters. It is the only register they have. They never greet anyone, never ask a question that is not about position, and express affection exclusively as unnecessarily precise navigation.

Casting: dry, low, unhurried. No accent that places them. Any gender.

**Sample lines.**

> First meeting: "Two days north of where you are standing. That is where you died. You walked further than most of them."

> Selling: "Three rooms, then a fourth that is not on anyone's map but mine. Forty flint."

> After a deeper run than last time: "Further. Six rooms further. I have marked it."

> When Alegus asks a personal question: "You are standing about a pace and a half too close."

> Very late, the only time the register slips, and it does not slip far: "Sit down. No. I mean it. Four minutes, then go."

**Arc.** Alegus works out what the table is somewhere around the Age of Sail and does not mention it, which is the kindest thing he does in the game. Their last conversation is the Cartographer giving him a route with no distance attached, because for the first time they do not know how far it goes.

## 2. The Archivist

**Function.** The codex. Tracks kills, discoveries, enemy and boss entries, and unlocks story beats as the record fills.

**Who.** They are keeping a complete account of a timeline that is being erased, and they are losing. They are made of the record: paper, layered, the most literally painted character in the game. They have never seen any of it. Everything they know is second-hand from people who came back, which makes them an authority with no first-hand knowledge, and they are perfectly aware of that.

**The voice hook: they cannot state a fact without citing what it contradicts.** Every sentence is a cross-reference. They correct Alegus constantly, and Alegus starts getting things wrong on purpose to give them something to do, which neither of them ever acknowledges.

Casting: precise, quick, faintly anxious, warmer than the words look on the page.

**Sample lines.**

> "That is not what it was called."

> "You said wall-man. The entry reads legionary. I have left both in."

> "Two accounts disagree about the colour of the sand. I have recorded that they disagree. That is also a fact."

> When Alegus brings back something new: "Oh. Oh, that is a new one. Say it again. Slower."

> Late, when the record starts failing: "There is no entry. I have looked. There has never before been no entry."

**Arc.** The tone plan turns their pedantry from irritating to comforting without changing the character at all. The same trait reads differently once Alegus has stopped finding the world funny, because by then being told the correct name for something is a small act of order. Their darkening beat is silence: an age arrives for which they have nothing.

---

## 3. Wren

**Function.** Rival and friend. Leaves marks in rooms, posts times, and occasionally fights alongside Alegus for one room.

**Who.** A runner from the Age of Sail, caught by the Loom the same way he was. She knows ships, rope, powder, and charts. This makes her a guide through the middle ages and completely useless past the Age of Revolution, which is deliberate: she stays funny into the dark ages precisely because she is as lost as he is there.

She beat him to the Terminus once, early, and will not let it go.

**The voice hook: total confidence, frequently wrong, never corrected.** She is the inverse of Alegus. He wonders at everything; she acts like she has seen it all. She explains things to him badly and at speed. She is also often wrong, just wrong in a different direction, and the two of them being confidently wrong together is most of the comedy.

Casting: fast, bright, a little rough. The most energy of anyone in the game.

**Sample lines.**

> "Twenty-two rooms. You?"

> Explaining: "That is a cannon. It is a tube you put anger in."

> On the Loom: "It caught me halfway down. Rude, honestly. I was busy."

> Losing a race: "I let you have that one. I am building your confidence. It is a whole thing I do."

> In a dark age, and this is the line the tone plan is built around: "Right. Well. I do not know what that one is either. Let us both not know it together."

**Arc.** She becomes the only friendship between two people from the same nowhere. Her importance rises as the ages darken, because once Alegus stops joking she is where the humour lives. By the late game the Hearth scenes are mostly her, and that is the plan working.

Her ten beats and in-run lines are written in `WREN_DIALOGUE.md`.

**Wren's marks.** Cheap, high-value flavour, and load-bearing for her arc: she carves her initial everywhere because she drowned in calm water and nobody noticed until morning.
 Some rooms carry a carved W and a scratched tally of her clear time. Finding one gives a small Ochre pickup and, rarely, a one-line voice barb left behind. It puts her in the run without modelling her into every room.

**The shared room.** Once every few runs she joins for a single room with a competitive kill count, and comments on the result afterwards at the Hearth. Alpha feature.

## 4. Keepsakes

A keepsake is a small passive, equipped at the Hearth and swappable at every age boundary during the re-forge. Each has three ranks that level with use, in the Hades pattern.

Fourteen at launch: five from the cast, nine from the Inventors.

### 4.1 Cast keepsakes

| From | Keepsake | Effect | Ranks |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Firekeeper | **The First Coal** | Heal 5 health at the start of each age | 5 / 8 / 12 |
| The Cartographer | **Dog-Ear** | Once per age, reroll the set of doors offered | 1 / 2 / 3 times |
| The Archivist | **The Tally** | Deal more damage to whichever enemy type you have killed most across all runs | 15% / 22% / 30% |
| Wren | **Wren's Knot** | Once per run, falling below 20% health grants 5 s of increased speed and damage | 20% / 30% / 40% |
| The Terminus | **The Quiet** | Rooms cleared without taking damage grant bonus Ochre. Obtained very late | 10 / 20 / 35 |

The Terminus keepsake exists because the final antagonist giving you a gift is the last beat of the story, and it should be mechanically small and thematically enormous.

### 4.2 Inventor keepsakes

One rule covers all nine, which is how Hades does it and it works:

**An Inventor's keepsake guarantees that Inventor appears at the first altar of the run, and adds a small passive in their theme.**

| Inventor | Passive |
|---|---|
| Ember | Burn lasts 1 s longer per rank |
| Wheel | Start each age with a full dash charge set and 10% move speed for the first room |
| Seed | Fountains heal an extra 10% per rank |
| Forge | The first heavy hit in each room Shatters |
| Glyph | The first enemy in each room is Marked |
| Lens | The first perfect dodge in each room refunds a dash |
| Salt | Death Defiance heals an extra 10% per rank |
| Compass | Door rewards for the next room are visible one room earlier |
| Powder | The first enemy killed in each room is Primed |

This makes keepsakes the tool for steering a build, which is the job they do best.

### 4.3 Rules

- One equipped at a time. Swappable only at an age boundary, inside the re-forge scene alongside the hold decision.
- Ranks level by use: a keepsake gains progress for each age completed while equipped.
- Cast keepsakes unlock through story beats, not purchase. Inventor keepsakes unlock on first taking three Inventions from that Inventor.
