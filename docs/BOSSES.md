# Launch bosses

One boss per age on the launch thread, plus the Terminus. Nine fights. Each entry covers the arena, phases, the lesson it teaches, and how it should feel. Mammoth Mother is detailed in `VERTICAL_SLICE.md` and summarised here.

Shared rules:

- Every boss has three phases at 100%, 65%, and 30%. Phase changes are visible: the arena or the boss physically changes.
- Every boss has one **tell** the player learns in phase 1 that becomes the kill window in phase 3.
- Every boss is beatable with any lineage and no Inventions. Inventions make it faster, never possible.
- Bosses do not dissolve like Frayed. Each has a bespoke ending that says something about the age.
- Boss arenas are the biggest rooms in their age and the most painterly. Budget them accordingly.

---

## 1. The Mammoth Mother (Stone Age, Ice Age)

**Lesson:** hit the weak points, and the world under you can change.
Frozen lake. Wounded, protecting an unseen calf. Spears in her flank are the tell. Phase 2 breaks the ice into floes and summons wolves. Phase 3 she stands her ground with overlapping area denial; hitting all four spears in 6 s staggers her. Full detail in the slice doc.

**Ending:** she kneels, the calf's call stops, the lake runs.

## 2. The Sphinx of Sand (Age of Rivers, Egypt)

**Lesson:** the arena is a puzzle and the boss is the clock.

**Arena:** a sunken temple court with a central obelisk and four pillars, each carved with a symbol. Sand pours in from the walls continuously. The floor slowly fills. Deep sand slows movement by half.

**Phase 1 (100 to 65):** the Sphinx sits on the obelisk and casts. Sand pillars erupt under Alegus with a 1 s telegraph. Every 15 s it asks a "riddle": one of the four pillars lights up, and Alegus must reach and strike it within 5 s or take a heavy unblockable hit. Striking it drains the sand level. The tell: it always looks at the pillar it is about to light.

**Phase 2 (65 to 30):** it comes down and fights in melee with paw swipes and a tail sweep, while the sand keeps rising. Riddles continue but now two pillars light and only the one it is looking at is real.

**Phase 3 (30 to 0):** it flies. It strafes the court dropping sand columns. The sand is now deep everywhere except on the pillar tops. The kill window: strike three real pillars in a row without being wrong, and it lands stunned for 4 s. Its glance is the tell from phase 1 and is now the whole fight.

**Ending:** it settles back onto the obelisk and lets the sand cover it, patiently, as if it has all the time in the world.

## 3. The Legate (Age of Empires, Rome)

**Lesson:** formations are one enemy with many bodies.

**Arena:** a circular training ground inside a legion camp, torches on posts, a tribunal platform at one end.

**Phase 1 (100 to 65):** the Legate does not fight. He commands. Twelve legionaries in a testudo advance on Alegus as one unit. The formation cannot be damaged from the front. Striking a flank breaks one shield and opens a gap. The Legate calls each manoeuvre 1 s before it happens (wheel left, advance, javelins), and his voice is the tell. Killing all twelve ends the phase.

**Phase 2 (65 to 30):** he steps down. Gladius and shield, fast and precise. He fights like an Edge lineage player: short combos and a parry. If Alegus attacks into his shield, he counters. Six more legionaries stand at the edges and throw javelins on his call.

**Phase 3 (30 to 0):** he calls the survivors into a ring around Alegus, shields in, and tightens it. The ring closes over 10 s and deals crushing damage if it completes. Alegus must break out through a flank, and the Legate always calls which side will weaken to let a soldier step in. Listening to the call is the kill window: break out where he said, and he is exposed for 3 s.

**Ending:** he sheathes his sword, orders the survivors to stand down, and they all salute an empty tribunal. The camp's torches go out one by one.

## 4. The Hollow Knight (Age of Faith and Steel, Crusader Europe)

**Lesson:** armour must be broken before damage matters.

**Arena:** a cathedral nave under construction, scaffolding on both sides, stained glass on the floor as a light pattern.

**Phase 1 (100 to 65):** an empty suit of plate animated by oaths. Fully armoured: all damage is reduced by 80% until a piece is broken. Each of five pieces (helm, both pauldrons, breastplate, greaves) has its own break threshold. Heavy hits break faster. Slow, telegraphed greatsword swings. The tell: it raises the sword to the side of the piece it will next protect with a parry.

**Phase 2 (65 to 30):** it climbs the scaffolding and rings the unfinished bell. The nave fills with Frayed pilgrims, unarmed and slow, who walk toward Alegus and explode into ink on contact. The Knight drops down for one swing every 8 s. Broken armour stays broken.

**Phase 3 (30 to 0):** it kneels in the light from the stained glass and begins to re-forge its armour, one piece every 6 s. Alegus must out-damage the repair. Pieces being repaired glow and take triple damage. The kill window is breaking a piece mid-repair, which stuns it for 2 s.

**Ending:** the armour collapses into its parts. There was never anything inside. Alegus's line: "Who was it for?"

## 5. The Admiral (Age of Sail, Caribbean)

**Lesson:** timing against a rhythm you cannot control.

**Arena:** the deck of a warship in a storm. The deck pitches on a 6 s cycle, which shifts the whole arena's slope. Loose cannon roll downhill. Broadsides from an unseen enemy ship hit the deck every 12 s in a telegraphed line.

**Phase 1 (100 to 65):** cutlass duel. The Admiral is fast, uses the pitch of the deck to lunge downhill, and fires a pistol once per pitch cycle. The tell: the pistol is always fired at the top of the roll.

**Phase 2 (65 to 30):** the mainmast falls and splits the deck in two. The Admiral takes the far side and fires swivel guns. Alegus must cross by the fallen mast during the level moment of the pitch. Crew Frayed board from the rails.

**Phase 3 (30 to 0):** the ship is sinking bow first. The deck tilt increases each cycle and does not fully return. The Admiral fights at the stern, the last level ground. Every broadside now hits the whole lower half of the deck. The kill window: the Admiral reloads at the top of each roll, and a hit during reload disarms the pistol for the rest of the fight.

**Ending:** the Admiral sits on the stern rail as the ship goes under and touches their hat. The sea takes the whole painting.

## 6. The Furnace (Age of Revolution, Foundry city)

**Lesson:** the environment kills enemies as well as you.

**Arena:** a foundry floor with three crucible channels, a rolling press, and a chain conveyor overhead.

**Phase 1 (100 to 65):** the Furnace is the building. There is no single body. Molten metal pours down the channels on a schedule and anything in a channel dies, including Frayed workers who spawn from the press. The player learns to bait enemies into channels. Three valves around the floor must be struck to slow the pours, and each valve struck deals damage to the Furnace.

**Phase 2 (65 to 30):** the conveyor starts. Hooks sweep the floor and carry off anything they catch, including Alegus, who takes damage and is dropped at the far end. Steam vents open with a 1 s telegraph. Valves now reset after 20 s.

**Phase 3 (30 to 0):** the Furnace heart opens in the far wall, a core of white heat. It is only exposed for 4 s after all three valves are struck within 10 s of each other. This is the kill window and it requires a route across all three channels during pours.

**Ending:** the heart cools and the whole foundry goes dark and silent. Frayed workers stop and sit down.

## 7. The Landship (Age of Wars, Trenches)

**Lesson:** cover matters, and some things cannot be fought head-on.

**Arena:** no man's land. Craters, wire, and a trench line at each end. Craters are cover: inside a crater, direct fire cannot hit Alegus.

**Phase 1 (100 to 65):** a tank crosses the arena at walking pace, firing a main gun with a 2 s telegraph and a sponson machine gun that tracks Alegus and forces movement between craters. Damage only lands on the rear engine plate. The tell: the engine plate glows before it turns, so the player learns to predict the turn.

**Phase 2 (65 to 30):** it bogs down in a crater. The rear plate is exposed but a gas cloud spreads from the wreck. Gas deals damage over time; craters fill with gas first. The safe ground is now the open, which the machine gun covers. Frayed infantry come over the wire.

**Phase 3 (30 to 0):** it frees itself and charges, crushing craters. Artillery falls in telegraphed columns across the whole arena. The kill window: after each charge it must reverse to turn, and the reverse exposes the plate for 3 s.

**Ending:** it stops. Its engine runs for a few seconds and dies. Nothing gets out of it. Alegus's line is the one from the bible: "You don't even have a face."

## 8. The Countdown (Age of Atoms and Stars, Silo)

**Lesson:** survival is the objective, not damage.

**Arena:** a missile silo, circular, with a central launch platform and four control consoles at the compass points.

There is no boss body. A 3 minute countdown starts. Frayed technicians and security spawn in waves. Every 30 s a klaxon marks a change: the platform vents fire, a blast door closes off one console, the floor rotates. The player must strike all four consoles to abort, but each console needs 5 s of uninterrupted holding, and enemies interrupt.

At 65% and 30% of the timer, all consoles reset. The final 30 s is the kill window: the security Frayed stop spawning and the technicians all run for the consoles to lock them. If the timer reaches zero the run ends, not just the fight.

**Ending:** the countdown stops at one. Every screen goes to paper white. It is the only boss room that ends in silence.

## 9. The Terminus

**Lesson:** everything you learned.

**Arena:** a white room. Unpainted paper with pencil underdrawing. Each phase paints one age into it.

**Phase 1 (100 to 65):** the Terminus is a figure drawn in a single ink line, Alegus's own height and build. It mirrors his lineage with the age 8 form. It fights with his exact moveset. The tell: it has no dash. It walks.

**Phase 2 (65 to 30):** it paints the room. Every 20 s the floor becomes an age (the Sphinx's pillars, the Legate's ring, the Admiral's pitching deck, the Landship's craters) and the corresponding boss's phase 3 mechanic runs for that window, in miniature, while the Terminus keeps fighting. Ages arrive in random order.

**Phase 3 (30 to 0):** the room goes white and silent. No music. The Terminus stops attacking and starts erasing: a slow expanding circle of blank paper from the centre. Anything in the blank is removed, including the floor. Alegus must stay on the painted edge and land hits while the safe ring shrinks. The kill window is the whole phase, but each hit is answered by an unblockable pulse that shrinks the ring faster. The player chooses their tempo.

**Ending:** the Terminus is not destroyed. It is pushed back. The room repaints from the edge inward, age by age, in order, ending with the Hearth's fire. Alegus wakes at the Hearth. The Firekeeper says the first line of the game again, and the game keeps going.
